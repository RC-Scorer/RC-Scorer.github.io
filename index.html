RC-Scorer

Standardized ride comfort prediction of candidate trajectories for autonomous driving without a vehicle model

Paper under review Code on release Split on release
Abstract Method Experiments Videos BibTeX

Abstract

Ride comfort is a primary measure of quality in autonomous driving services. Yet the comfort measures used in autonomous driving consider only longitudinal and lateral motion, leaving out the vertical vibration the road transmits. They also judge motion by instantaneous thresholds such as jerk rather than by a standardized evaluation of the whole trajectory. ISO 2631-1 provides such an evaluation, weighting body acceleration on all three axes by frequency and adding measures for sudden jolts, but computing it before a trajectory is driven has required a vehicle dynamics model calibrated to each vehicle. This paper proposes RC-Scorer, a Transformer-based deep learning model trained on driving logs alone that reads the road under a candidate trajectory and predicts its weighted acceleration waveform on all three axes. The resulting ISO 2631-1 evaluation values are more accurate than those of a calibrated quarter-car combined with the trajectory kinematics, a road-roughness index regression and a learned baseline. Deployed on an autonomous vehicle, RC-Scorer scores every candidate from a lidar road profile built while driving, showing that it can run in the planning loop; in test scenarios where obstacles can be fully avoided, the measured ISO 2631-1 vibration total value over them fell by 75–84 %.

Method

No vehicle model anywhere in the chain

A causal mask stops future road geometry from explaining present vibration. The encoder carries no explicit vehicle representation and no vehicle-specific calibration: one learned model predicts all three axes jointly, in place of a calibrated vehicle model for the vertical axis and trajectory kinematics for the horizontal ones.

Scored per candidate, in the loop

Each planning cycle the road grid is re-sampled under every candidate's own wheel paths, and the planner adds the predicted av to its centerline, consistency and curvature costs. The loss puts the reported quantity in the objective: a waveform term, a relative band-energy term and a moment term on the av distribution.

Experiments

Offline

CARD, a multi-country road dataset giving four-wheel contact-point height, the planned trajectory and synchronised IMU together. The held-out split is 29 sequences and 13,875 windows, grouped by trajectory overlap so no road appears on both sides. All methods share one weighting implementation and identical wheel-path inputs.

Closed loop

An autonomous Hyundai Ioniq 5 with a roof-mounted 128-channel lidar and an RTK-GNSS/INS for ground truth. A lattice planner emits five candidates at lateral offsets 0, ±1.25 and ±2.5 m. RC-Scorer reads the road profile the vehicle accumulates as it drives, not the post-processed one it was trained on.

Weighting the target alone hurts — no loss term reads the spectral distribution it changes — and the band log-ratio term is what reads it. Removing the road input instead and retraining raises the av error from 0.0449 to 0.0726, and all three axes worsen, so the horizontal prediction draws on the road and not on speed and curvature alone. Simply holding the weighted RMS of the preceding 2 s scores 0.1384: the accuracy comes from the road ahead rather than from extending the vibration just measured.

The three axes are given beside av so a reduction produced by the road can be told from one produced by the manoeuvre; the weighted vertical peak falls by 88 % to 94 % in the full-avoidance scenarios. In S5 both runs keep their wheels clear and the planner selects the center in all 75 recorded cycles; in S6 the enabled run shifts left by 0.47 m on average. Scoring five candidates takes 88–109 ms on a Jetson AGX Orin, of which the network itself is 7–9 ms.

Limitations

Each cell is one disabled and one enabled run, so the reductions are point estimates, and only the nominal 15 km h−1 setting is reported. No alternative comfort cost is run in closed loop, so the reductions are not attributed to RC-Scorer over such costs. Untraversed candidates have no measured outcome, so the predicted ordering is not validated by the observed path changes, and the offline split is lower in amplitude than the obstacle zones. Offline, no convolutional or recurrent baseline is run and the MLP is untuned; checkpoints and the quarter-car calibration are selected on the validation split, which all methods follow alike.
