What this page holds

This page accompanies a submission on predicting the ride comfort of the candidate trajectories produced by a multi-modal motion planner, so that predicted comfort can act as one of the scores deciding which trajectory the vehicle follows. The model reads the road surface ahead and estimates what each candidate path would feel like before the vehicle commits to it.

Everything below is raw evidence from the real-vehicle campaign: the test site, the scenario protocol, and every recording taken, including the runs that failed. Nothing has been cut for presentation.

Author and affiliation details are withheld while the paper is under review.

Test site

All runs took place at K-City, an autonomous-driving proving ground in Hwaseong-si, Republic of Korea. Two sections were used.

Protocol

Each scenario is driven twice at every target speed. The two runs differ only in the planner's cost function, so the pair isolates what the comfort term changes.

Baseline

wcomfort = 0

The comfort term is switched off. The planner selects on its usual costs alone.

Comfort

wcomfort > 0

The predicted comfort score is restored and enters trajectory selection.

Three-axis acceleration and angular rate are logged throughout both runs; the comparison between the two logs is the measurement. Every attempt was recorded and kept, successful or not.

Scenarios

Five structured scenarios on the straight section, three additional ones elsewhere on the site.
