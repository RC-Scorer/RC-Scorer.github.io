Scenarios the score has to separate

A comfort score is only useful if it ranks candidate trajectories the way a passenger would. The eight scenarios below were designed to make that ranking observable: in each one the planner faces a choice between paths that differ in what the vehicle would feel, and the correct answer is known in advance.

The diagrams show the two paths the scenario contrasts — the one a planner takes on its usual costs alone, and the one a comfort-aware planner should prefer. Five are driven on the straight section, three elsewhere on the site. They are described here rather than in the paper, where there was no room for them.

Experiments

Every scenario was driven on a real vehicle at a proving ground, twice at each target speed, and logged in full.

Test site

All runs took place at K-City, an autonomous-driving proving ground in Hwaseong-si, Republic of Korea. Two sections were used.

Protocol

Each scenario is driven twice at every target speed. The two runs differ only in the planner's cost function, so the pair isolates what the comfort term changes.

Baseline

wcomfort = 0

The comfort term is switched off. The planner selects on its usual costs alone.

Comfort

wcomfort > 0

The predicted comfort score is restored and enters trajectory selection.

Three-axis acceleration and angular rate are logged throughout both runs; the comparison between the two logs is the measurement. Every attempt was recorded and kept, successful or not.
