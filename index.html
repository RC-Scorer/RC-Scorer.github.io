Abstract

Ride comfort is largely settled at the moment a trajectory is chosen, yet autonomous driving planners score it with threshold tests on acceleration and jerk, with no frequency weighting and no reference to a vibration standard. Reaching ISO 2631-1 quantities instead requires survey-grade road input together with a per-vehicle identified dynamics model, neither of which production onboard perception provides. RC-Scorer predicts ISO 2631-1 frequency-weighted body acceleration from onboard wheel-path road observations and ordinary driving logs, with no vehicle dynamics model and no parameter identification in the loop. The method has three components: a causal Transformer over the road geometry a candidate trajectory would traverse, an objective targeting the reported weighted RMS, and a frequency-response probe that tests what the network represents. Using only a causal onboard reconstruction, RC-Scorer predicts av 2.2 times more accurately than a calibrated quarter-car given the survey-grade ground-truth surface, and reduces measured weighted vertical acceleration by up to 95 % in closed loop on a production vehicle.

Method

Scored per candidate, not per road

At each planning cycle the road grid is re-sampled under each candidate's own wheel paths and the predictor re-run. The road encoder output is shared across candidates, so the marginal cost of one more candidate is small.

The road as perception emits it

Forward integration differentiates the profile twice, so the physical route needs a road estimate no production stack produces. The road channel is taken with its dropout, noise and precision loss intact; gaps are filled by linear interpolation, 1.8 mm error for gaps of 14 samples or fewer.

Causality in the architecture

Future road geometry cannot cause present vibration, and a causal mask over self-attention enforces that rather than leaving it to the data. The model carries no explicit vehicle representation — no latent embedding, no conditioning modulation, no identified parameters.

Training on the quantity that is reported

A time-domain squared error shrinks predicted amplitude structurally: at the least-squares solution the regression slope converges to the squared correlation. The objective therefore adds a relative band-energy term and a moment term on the av distribution.

ISO 2631-1 reduction

Each axis waveform is filtered by the weighting the standard prescribes for that direction — Wd horizontal, Wk vertical — and combined into av. VDV is reported alongside the RMS because 14.66 % of windows exceed the standard's shock criterion.

A probe for what was learned

Output error cannot separate learned dynamics from memorised statistics. The road-to-body frequency response is compared against measurement and a fitted quarter-car, stratified by speed: wheelbase filtering moves the body-mode peak, and a fixed-parameter model cannot follow it.

Obstacle layouts for the closed-loop test

Six layouts, each putting a different question to the scorer. S1–S3 place speed bumps under one or both wheel tracks. S4 places bumps of unequal height at the same longitudinal station, so no candidate avoids both and the correct behaviour is to move toward the lower one. S5 is a pothole field. S6 places the obstacles between the wheel tracks, where lateral motion is the wrong answer.

Experiments

Part of the quantitative evaluation is being finalized. The component ablation and the two analysis figures are awaiting the retrained checkpoints. The offline accuracy table and the closed-loop table below are measured on the final data.

Setup

Offline

A multi-country road dataset giving four-wheel contact-point road height, the planned trajectory and synchronised IMU together; the official validation split is 29 sequences and 13,875 windows. Two road conditions are evaluated throughout — Road A, the offline contact-point ground truth, and Road B, reconstructed online from only the LiDAR returns acquired up to t0, which is what a production stack has at decision time.

Closed loop

A production battery-electric SUV. A roof-mounted 128-beam lidar at 10 Hz is accumulated into a ground-anchored grid; ground truth is a 100 Hz navigation-grade inertial unit. The lattice planner emits five candidates at lateral offsets 0, ±1.25 and ±2.5 m, with a 0.15 hysteresis margin, a 0.3 m deadband and a 0.26 m per-cycle rate limit. Cost age at the planner is 49 ms median against a 0.4 s gate.

Vehicle conditioning is reported in text because its conclusion is negative: six ways of supplying an explicit vehicle representation — latent embeddings of dimension 4 and 64, FiLM, conditional LayerNorm and per-token SPADE — all degraded held-out accuracy while training loss fell monotonically with capacity. The embedding identifies which training run a window came from, and a held-out run is by construction one the model has never identified.

Test site

Every paired run was driven on the same course at a proving ground. Two sections were used: a straight multi-lane section for the bump layouts, and a narrow walled corridor for the pothole field.

Closed loop on a production vehicle

A paired design: for each layout the vehicle drove the same course twice, scorer disabled and enabled, at two speed settings (S5 at the lower setting only), giving eleven pairs. In all eleven the struck interval and the passed interval occupy the same longitudinal range, so the pairs compare the same geometry. The judged quantity is aw,z rather than av, because the vibration total value mixes in the manoeuvre that avoidance itself produces.

S4 behaves as designed

With no clean candidate the planner moves toward the lower obstacle. The residual 53–61 % reduction is the difference between striking a low bump and a high one, not a failure to avoid.

S6 carries the most weight

With the obstacles between the wheel tracks the correct behaviour is to do nothing. The scorer selected the center in 92 % and 91 % of cycles and the planner applied it in 99 %. Over the approach the five candidates' predicted aw,z spread from 0.15 to 0.40 m s−2 with the center at the bottom: the model is scoring the center as the best option, not being restrained from moving.

Limitations

Each cell is a single run, so the reductions are point estimates, and in the higher-speed cells the enabled run traversed the obstacle more slowly, conflating avoidance with slowing; the claim rests on the speed-matched pairs. The cost of avoiding is not quantified. Where a wheel strikes an obstacle the prediction is low by a factor of two to four, though candidate ordering — what selection depends on — survives. The horizontal axes hold offline but not in deployment, which is why aw,z and not av is the closed-loop criterion. Deployment is shown, necessity is not: the results establish that a physical-unit comfort prediction changes the chosen trajectory, not that ISO frequency weighting is required for that change.
